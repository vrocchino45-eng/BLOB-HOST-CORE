## Ownership and interpretation

- The renderer had 27 resident linked programs. Typical direct calls spent five offscreen draws (four opaque, one transmissive), six main opaque/transmissive draws and about 382–384 main transparent draws. `MeshStandardMaterial` and `MeshBasicMaterial` made up most direct calls. No per-shader GPU elapsed measurement is available. Material classes, offscreen and main direct submissions live inside the whole render interval. Driver waits and blending can contribute. The asynchronous WebGL timer covered the whole scene submission, including nested Three.js work, and its 71 valid results per run overlap CPU execution.
- `src/core/SystemRegistry.js::update` elapsed includes children. Native telemetry and HUD totals overlap their child timings. Timings are for entire rendered frames and include catch-up substeps. Silt and Growth were the largest consistent registry children. This says where to look first and does not isolate their geometry updates from other per-step work.
- 301 visible and 549 effectively hidden meshes (368/692 total nodes) were observed at the sampling boundary. Thirty regional owners were already skipped, including Vascular/Grave. A hidden object's presence in the scene graph does not establish that it rendered. Matrix traversal was about 1–2 ms at the median across repeats.
- CDP traces showed minor collections (approximately 2.4–5.2 ms each). Parent `MinorGC` and nested `V8.GCScavenger` spans overlap; add neither to CPU totals. Allocation sampling implicated Three.js/native code first, followed by `CoreEmbodimentSystem`, the Silt route, `RenderBudgetSystem`, `LiftFieldReadabilitySystem` and `CameraReadabilitySystem`. The estimated sampled sizes include reclaimed objects and probe overhead. They are not retained heap or leak measurements.
- PF02 compared ordinary, Director, bot, telemetry and capture paths with broader overhead instrumentation. PF03 controls those toggles and separately records actual render submissions and registry children. Both establish that successful mechanics can coexist with uneven frame cadence. Comparing their medians directly as a speedup would mix workloads, capture conditions and probes. Run 2 was notably slower despite similar drawing geometry and pose. Background browser/installer activity could not be excluded across runs.

## Next experiments

1. Split transparent calls by authored visual group. Measure a development-only A/B that omits one presentation group at a time and preserves contact cues, scene transforms and gameplay snapshots. Remeasure frame-tail and GPU time with low overhead and a matched visual capture. This is the highest signal because transparent submissions dominated all measured runs.
2. In a separate controlled probe, partition the Silt and Growth geometry/snapshot updates and trace transient allocations. Consider reuse only after confirming stable retained state, published snapshots, and same-step ordering. Matrix traversal and HUD redesign have weaker evidence here.
3. Keep untimed control runs and synchronous-frame visual review outside the sampling run. No optimization, quality changes, simulation changes or Experience verdict were applied.
