# HC-029C Focused Playtest — Verb Composition Grammar

## Goal

Determine whether the three existing verbs now feel like a composable language on one retained relationship rather than independent abilities stacked on the same buttons.

Recommended session: **10–15 minutes**. Use a space where two or more chemistry objects can be compared, but spend most of the test manipulating one retained target.

## Controls under test

- **X** — Pulse (fires on release)
- **RT** — Absorb
- **LT** — Stabilize
- **Right stick** — intentional relationship redirection

## 1. Single-verb regression

Use X, RT and LT separately first.

Expected: HC029B still feels intact. Relationship retention, release and handoff should not feel different merely because HC029C exists.

## 2. LT + X — controlled push

Establish one target. Hold LT until Stabilize visibly begins organizing it. While LT remains held, hold and release X.

Look for:
- the retained target receives the deliberate Pulse response;
- nearby objects still acknowledge the broad inquiry but less strongly;
- the focused target moves with less chaotic recoil than Pulse alone;
- Stabilize does not lose or silently change the target.

Question: **Does this feel like “hold coherent, then push” without needing an explanation?**

## 3. LT + RT — coherent draw

Hold LT and RT on the same retained target.

Look for:
- the object continues drawing inward;
- rotation/helix aggression is visibly calmer;
- absorption progress still advances rather than freezing;
- the Stabilize field and Absorb transfer look like one relationship.

Question: **Does this feel more controlled than RT alone, or merely slower?**

## 4. RT + X — tension / exchange

Hold RT, then hold/release X while RT stays down. Try both a quick Pulse and a charged Pulse.

Expected:
- the object should not receive full outward Pulse and full inward Absorb simultaneously;
- incorporation briefly slows;
- the relationship reads as being pulled in opposite directions;
- quick tension clears sooner than charged tension.

Question: **Can you feel a temporary push/pull tension state rather than input conflict?**

## 5. LT + RT + X — equilibrium

Hold LT + RT, then hold/release X without releasing the triggers immediately.

Expected:
- target motion becomes strongly restrained;
- the relationship remains active and readable;
- the state does not become an explosive “ultimate”; 
- endurance reserve should fall faster than with a single held verb.

Question: **Does this feel like effortful equilibrium—useful but costly—rather than a reward for mashing all buttons?**

## 6. Release grammar

From each combination, release only one input at a time.

Expected examples:
- LT+RT → release LT → ordinary Absorb
- LT+RT → release RT → ordinary Stabilize
- RT+X tension → Pulse envelope fades → ordinary held Absorb
- full system → release LT → brief Pulse+Absorb tension, then held Absorb as Pulse decays

Transitions should feel continuous rather than snapping between unrelated modes.

## 7. Relationship integrity

With a second object nearby, repeat combinations and deliberately move the right stick between objects.

Expected: composition never invents its own target. HC029A relationship handoff remains the only target authority.

## What to report

The most useful notes are simple:

- **LT+X:** controlled / too weak / still chaotic
- **LT+RT:** coherent / just slow / hard to read
- **RT+X:** tension / feels broken / too brief / too sticky
- **all three:** equilibrium / useless / too strong / reserve cost too high or too low
- any moment where the visible relationship and physical result disagree
